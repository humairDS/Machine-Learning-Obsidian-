LLM stands for **Large Language Model**.

It is a type of artificial intelligence trained on vast amounts of text to understand and generate human language. LLMs can:

- Answer questions
- Write and summarize text
- Translate languages
- Generate code
- Assist with research and brainstorming
- Hold conversations
    

Examples of LLMs include:

- OpenAI's GPT models (such as ChatGPT)
- Google's Gemini models
- Anthropic's Claude models
- Meta's Llama models
    

### Why is it called "Large"?

- **Large**: Trained on enormous datasets and contains billions or trillions of parameters (internal numerical values learned during training).
    
- **Language**: Works primarily with human language (text, and sometimes speech or images).
    
- **Model**: A mathematical system that learns patterns from data.
    

### Simple analogy

Think of an LLM as a highly advanced autocomplete system. Given some text, it predicts what words are likely to come next—but because it has learned from massive amounts of information, it can produce useful explanations, conversations, code, and creative content.

For example, if you ask:

> "Why is the sky blue?"

An LLM can generate an explanation about how sunlight scatters in Earth's atmosphere, even though it isn't "looking up" the answer the way a search engine does.
